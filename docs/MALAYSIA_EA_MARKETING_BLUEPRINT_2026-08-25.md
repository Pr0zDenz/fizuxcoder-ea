# FizuxCoder Malaysia Marketing Blueprint

> **Financial marketing notice.** This plan is marketing analysis, not financial or legal advice. Automated trading involves substantial risk, and no creative should imply a guaranteed return, income, or capital protection. Confirm Malaysian regulatory requirements and Meta eligibility before any paid campaign is launched.

## 1. Strategic position

FizuxCoder should be positioned as a **risk-first MT5 automation software provider**, not as a managed-investment, signal-selling, wealth-building, or “easy income” offer. The most credible point of differentiation is the operating discipline already present in the product: protected delivery, verified receipt claims, account binding, documented installation, visible risk language, and a defined licence workflow.

The market objective is not to create urgency around trading. It is to attract Malaysian MT5 users who are willing to evaluate system setup, understand execution risk, and use a demo environment before any live deployment. This approach materially reduces the risk of message mismatch with the product’s own risk-first website and aligns with the need to avoid deceptive financial promotion.[1][2]

| Message to lead with | Supporting proof on the site | Claim to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Study the workflow before you configure it.” | System comparison, setup worksheet, installation guides, risk notes. | “Set and forget income.” |
| “MT5 automation with documented setup and account-bound access.” | Portal receipt verification, MT5 binding, protected package delivery. | “Guaranteed access to profitable trades.” |
| “Demo-first configuration and clear operating steps.” | Installation guides and WebRequest instructions. | “Works for every broker, market, or account.” |
| “Choose the system whose setup you understand.” | Gemini/3S product distinction and transparent licence terms. | “Best bot in Malaysia” or unsubstantiated superiority claims. |

## 2. Malaysia-focused acquisition funnel

DataReportal reports high digital penetration in Malaysia—35.4 million internet users and 30.7 million social-media user identities in late 2025—supporting a mobile-first discovery path, while its methodology cautions against treating platform estimates as unique engaged users.[3] The recommended launch is therefore an **organic learning loop first**, followed by a limited paid test only if legal and platform review permit it.

| Funnel stage | Customer question | Channel and asset | Primary call to action | Evidence of quality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery | “What is this EA actually designed to do?” | Threads text posts, short vertical educational videos, simple workflow graphics. | Read the system briefing. | Qualified landing-page visits; 50% video views. |
| Evaluation | “Which product and workflow apply to me?” | Landing-page comparison, setup worksheet, product-specific guide. | Compare Gemini and 3S. | Product-comparison engagement; guide opens. |
| Trust | “What happens after I pay?” | Receipt-claim explainer, protected-download and MT5-binding walkthrough. | Review the portal workflow. | Portal-guide engagement; not direct-message volume. |
| Conversion | “Can I proceed responsibly?” | Product page with clear price, licence terms, risk disclosure, and direct checkout. | Choose a product only after reviewing the risk notice. | Verified claim completion, never a promise of trading outcome. |
| Retention | “How do I install and maintain access?” | Buyer activation email, README, documented support/renewal path. | Complete demo-first installation. | Successful protected download and support-resolution time. |

## 3. Content pillars for Threads

| Pillar | Purpose | Recommended weekly cadence | Safe examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Automation literacy** | Explain what an EA can and cannot do. | 2 posts | “An EA executes rules; it does not remove market risk.” |
| **Setup transparency** | Demonstrate the practical MT5 and portal workflow. | 2 posts | “Four checks before attaching an EA to a demo chart.” |
| **Risk discipline** | Reinforce demo-first, broker/account responsibility, and position sizing. | 1 post | “A setup checklist is more useful than a profit screenshot.” |
| **Product architecture** | Differentiate Gemini and 3S without performance claims. | 1 post | “Gemini and 3S have different activation flows—choose based on your operating setup.” |
| **Build-in-public operations** | Humanize support, release process, and version clarity. | 1 post | “Why protected delivery and MT5 account binding exist.” |

Threads is appropriate for the first organic test because Meta documents support for text, image, video, carousel, links, replies, and insights through its API.[4] Keep individual posts under the documented 500-character text limit and use no more than one simple destination link per post.[5]

## 4. Paid-media boundary

The initial paid-media recommendation is **prepare only, do not launch**. Meta’s financial-services policy says it may require verification and regulatory authorization for financial-product or service advertising, and it expressly prohibits ads promoting CFD trading.[6] Because the EA’s intended trading use may involve forex/CFD environments, the safest practical route is to request written legal/platform review before creating or funding any Meta campaign.

If written clearance confirms that an education/brand campaign is acceptable, test only a low-risk **education-first** concept that routes to the public system briefing and risk disclosure—not directly to a checkout page, deposit path, direct-message conversation, or lead form requesting financial data. The campaign should exclude claims about returns and should target adults only, subject to the exact options Meta makes available in the account.

| Readiness gate | Requirement | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal and regulatory classification | Confirm whether marketing the EA, referral, and target instruments requires any Malaysian authorization, disclosure, or restriction. | Owner with qualified Malaysian legal/regulatory reviewer. |
| Meta advertiser readiness | Confirm business verification, category eligibility, and whether the intended creative/landing page is eligible. | Owner in Meta Business Manager. |
| Content review | Approve every claim, visual, caption, landing URL, risk statement, and language version. | Owner. |
| Measurement readiness | Confirm UTM naming and page analytics before any organic link is published. | Owner/operator. |
| Spend authorization | Approve objective, daily cap, dates, audience, and stop rule in writing. | Owner, per campaign. |

## 5. Measurement and stop rules

Use a small scorecard that rewards qualified education rather than raw reach. No automated action should increase budget, create an ad, or publish a post without a recorded owner approval.

| Metric | What it indicates | Initial decision use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualified landing-page sessions | Discovery interest after excluding obvious bot traffic. | Compare post themes and creative hooks. |
| Guide/open-workflow engagement | Willingness to understand setup before purchase. | Prioritize educational content that produces deeper consideration. |
| Verified receipt claims | Legitimate completed product journeys. | Measure purchase-flow quality; never treat it as trading performance. |
| Support requests by topic | Setup friction and content gaps. | Create FAQ posts and guide improvements. |
| Policy warnings, rejected ads, or user reports | Compliance risk. | Pause all related content/ads; review before any restart. |
